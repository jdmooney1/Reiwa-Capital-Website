Direction B — read first

The Mandate Brief

Second design direction for reiwa-capital.com · wireframes to the right (desktop row, mobile row, state row) · no production code — for approval

1 · The direction in one paragraph

Each page is a concise institutional briefing: the reader is shown a compact overview, selects the subject — a market, a process stage, a strategy — and the page replaces content in place instead of scrolling through it. Orientation is persistent (a quiet section rail on desktop), density is controlled, and detail sits behind disclosure. Heroes, header, palette, DM Sans, drawer, EN/JA toggle, next-page rail and the purple footer are untouched — the direction applies only below each hero.

It should feel like a well-prepared investment briefing — calm, precise, immediately understandable. It must never feel like software, a dashboard, a portal, a consultancy deck, a sales brochure, an agency site or a luxury brand.

2 · Information system

  • Principle: a visitor understands each page without opening any disclosure. Selected states carry the primary facts; disclosure carries technical depth.
  • Components (the full set): positioning statement · selected-state visual panel · responsibility matrix · process lane · criteria matrix · expandable technical detail · image caption · compact client/market index. Spacing, alignment and thin rules only — no bordered cards, no icons, no pills.
  • Desktop orientation rail: ~2 of 12 columns, text + thin line, highlights the section in view, sticky only where useful. It is orientation, not navigation. Mobile replaces it with a compact horizontal section index under the hero — never a fixed secondary bar.
  • Selected states: stronger text colour + short underline; unselected labels drop opacity slightly; image and content replace in place (image crossfade 180–240ms, content 140–180ms). No filled tabs, no sliding indicators, no zooms. Understandable with motion off.

5 · Visible by default vs behind interaction

Visible by default

  • Home: hero, ACQUIRE / STRUCTURE / OVERSEE sequence with one line each, three strategy routes.
  • About: positioning statement; investor-objective / Reiwa-role pair; the SELECTED market's photograph + three labelled groups; client index + support line.
  • Approach: full responsibility matrix (both lanes, all ten items), the retained-with-investor statement, the five-stage track, the selected stage's objective + considerations.
  • Focus: strategy selector, selected strategy's image + thesis + three criteria, the five-line assessment index.

Behind one interaction (selection)

  • The other market's briefing; the other four stages' objectives; the other two strategies' theses and criteria.

Behind disclosure (expand)

  • About: "Market detail" — the four supporting market notes per city (existing approved copy).
  • Approach: per-stage "Detail" — the existing approved stage paragraphs, verbatim.
  • Focus: "What we look for" — the existing approved Asset Types / Underwriting fields, verbatim.

Nothing is removed from the site. Content removed from view is exactly the duplication already flagged in the audit: the Home lifecycle repetition (Approach carries it) and the Focus intro that repeated its own subhead.

6 · Accessibility behaviour

  • Selectors are real <button>s with aria-selected (tab semantics only on Focus, where the pattern genuinely behaves as tabs); stage/market panels are named regions; disclosure uses native details/summary.
  • Keyboard: all states reachable by Tab/Enter/Space; focus moves only on mobile market switch (as production already does); no hover-dependent information.
  • Selected state survives EN/JA switching (state lives outside the i18n sweep, as production market selection already does).
  • Print and no-JS: ALL states render expanded in sequence — a printed page exposes every market, stage and strategy. No core information depends on interaction.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: crossfades and underline movement disabled; replacement is instant.

7 · Risks to institutional credibility

  1. Software drift. Selectors + rails can read as an app. Control: text-only controls, thin rules, no fills, no motion flourishes, generous whitespace.
  2. Comparison cost. Selected states prevent seeing all three strategies side by side — a diligence reader comparing strategies must click three times. Mitigation: print view exposes all; criteria vocabulary is identical across strategies.
  3. Perceived thinness. ~35 visible words on Focus can read as light for a £100m+ mandate audience if the disclosure affordances are too quiet. Mitigation: the assessment index keeps analytical depth visible.
  4. Briefing tone → consultancy tone. Matrices and tracks flirt with deck language. Control: no icons, no numbers-as-decoration, institutional captions.
  5. Photography demoted. One image per page at a time weakens the two-city visual argument About currently makes with both cards visible.

9 · Recommendation

The Quiet Dossier remains the better primary direction for Reiwa Capital — with two Mandate Brief components adopted into it: the responsibility matrix and the criteria/assessment vocabulary.

Reasoning: the audience is introduction-led and diligence-minded. They read carefully, compare, print, and forward pages to advisers. A linear editorial page serves that behaviour natively; selected states serve time-poor repeat visitors, which this audience is not. Selection also makes the site's strongest asset — restrained photography of two cities — take turns instead of building a cumulative impression. The Mandate Brief's genuine advances (the responsibility matrix's clarity about custody and decision-making, the criteria matrices, the disciplined word budgets) transfer cleanly into the Dossier's linear flow, and have already been proven in the Rev. 3 prototypes. Direction B standalone is the right choice only if analytics later show substantial repeat/desk-research traffic that wants reference speed over narrative.

10 · Approved copy used

All wireframe copy is existing approved copy or copy supplied in the brief (ACQUIRE/STRUCTURE/OVERSEE lines, identity pair, client index). No performance figures, AUM, volumes, track-record years, strategy claims, permissions or client references are invented anywhere in this direction.
