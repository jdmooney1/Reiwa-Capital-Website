Anti-Template Visual Audit

Final pass, applying one test to every element: could this appear substantially unchanged on a luxury hotel, architecture studio, design consultancy or generic property-investment template? No website changes made. Methodology note: the live preview iframe would not settle for on-page screenshots this session, so composition/typography/colour findings are traced to exact source rules and computed contrast ratios (cited below) rather than pixel captures — the one category needing pixel evidence, photography, was checked by opening the actual image files directly, which is the more decisive test for that category anyway.

Audit only · no changes made

Headline finding — read this one first

All four hero images (Home, About, Approach, Investment Focus) are blurred at the source to the point of being partly or wholly unrecognisable as architecture. This was confirmed two ways: opening the files directly, and checking their data weight — about-hero-1600.webp is a full 1600×900 frame at just 11KB; home-hero-blur.webp is 1300×1950 at 49KB. A sharp photograph at these dimensions normally runs 150–300KB+; this little data means the image itself contains almost no fine detail, at the file level, not a display-time CSS blur. By contrast, the two market photographs on About (market-london-1537.webp, market-amsterdam-1562.webp) are sharp, specific and excellent — real, identifiable skylines. Every page's first impression is currently its weakest image. See Photography §5 for the full finding.

1Typography

T1RequiredHero location caption is a hospitality-template signature move

What is wrong

"Tokyo · London · Amsterdam" sits centred, tracked, uppercase, under the H1 — .home-hero .loc { text-align:center; text-transform:uppercase; letter-spacing:var(--type-label-ls); }.

Why it feels generic

A centred, tracked-caps, middot-separated city list under a display headline is one of the single most recognisable conventions in five-star hotel and hospitality-group homepages ("NEW YORK · LONDON · TOKYO"). It's the exact silhouette the brief explicitly warns against ("must not... feel like a hotel website"), independent of the separate centring bug already logged against this element.

Smallest effective correction

Left-align it (already required as a plain bug fix) and drop the uppercase transform — let it sit as a quiet, sentence-case caption rather than a tracked-caps banner line. The information (three cities) can stay exactly as is.

What must be preserved

The three-city fact itself, and its position under the sub-line — this is real, useful information, not filler.

Risk of over-correcting

None — this is a typographic-treatment change only, not a content or layout change.

T2RecommendedHome's H1 is hard-coded into four forced lines

What is wrong

data-en="European<br>Real Estate.<br>Structured for<br>Overseas Capital." — four manual breaks dictate the exact line shape regardless of viewport.

Why it feels generic

Manually authored line breaks (rather than a max-width and natural wrap) are brittle by nature — they were tuned for one width and stop being a deliberate choice the moment the viewport, font substitution, or copy changes.

Smallest effective correction

Confirm whether "Structured for / Overseas Capital." is a deliberate two-line rhetorical pairing (worth keeping as an authored break) or an artefact of tuning for one screen size (worth removing in favour of the existing max-width:16ch doing the wrapping naturally).

What must be preserved

The headline's overall short-line, generous-leading rhythm — whichever way this is resolved, it shouldn't run as a single long line.

Risk of over-correcting

Removing breaks that were in fact a deliberate rhetorical pairing would flatten a considered piece of writing into a merely-technical wrap. Confirm intent before changing.

T3Leave unchangedPurple-on-cream contrast

Computed directly: --eyebrow-ink #6E5A78 on cream ≈ 5.9:1; on-dark muted lavender text ≈ 8.4:1. Both comfortably clear AA, one close to AAA. The in-code comments on --ink-3/--ink-4 ("lifted to ≥4.5:1", "lifted for separators") show this was already deliberately remediated once. No low-contrast purple-on-cream problem found.

T4Leave unchangedNo excessively thin text is possible

Only two weights are shipped sitewide — DM Sans 400 and 500. No 300/200 face is declared or loaded anywhere, so an "oversized lightweight statement" can't currently occur.

T5OptionalOne content formula — number, heading, one sentence — recurs across four different sections

Process stages, Assessment criteria, Home's role routes and Investment Focus's chapter openers all reduce to the same primitive. Each instance is well-executed and appropriately restrained on its own, and each already varies its treatment somewhat (rail+node vs. baseline-adjacent vs. differing scale) — so this isn't a per-instance defect. Named only because, read in one sitting across three pages, sixteen numbered items in total is worth being aware of as a rhythm. No correction proposed — inventing variety for its own sake would be the wrong fix.

2Composition

C1RecommendedThe brand-symbol watermark is decoration standing in for a diagram

What is wrong

.reiwa-geo — a large outline of the brand mark — sits behind four sections (Home positioning, About identity, Investment Focus assessment, Contact body) and conveys no information.

Why it feels generic

An abstract line-art brand squiggle bleeding off the edge of a section is close to the single most common design-consultancy template flourish there is — it reads as "we needed something back there" rather than as content.

Smallest effective correction

Either remove it, or give it an explicit, stated job (a genuine watermark/security-paper association tied to the brand mark's real proportions) rather than leaving it as ambient decoration. Contrast with the Operating Model's axis line one section away on Approach — that line is diagram-as-information (it encodes the coordination relationship); this mark is diagram-as-decoration. The site already has one correct example next to one incorrect one.

What must be preserved

Nothing depends on it — no information is currently carried by this element.

Risk of over-correcting

None; removing pure ornament carries no downside.

C2Leave unchangedEqual-width column grids checked, all carry genuinely equal content

Home's role-row (3 items), areas-band (3 items), About's client grid (4 items), Investment Focus's assessment grid (5 items) — spot-checked word counts per item within each grid; none forces meaningfully unequal information into a mechanically equal frame. The Operating Model deliberately breaks equal-column treatment (1fr / 1.22fr / 1fr, the centre column also offset higher) specifically to avoid this — a good, deliberate anti-template move from this session's rebuild.

C3Leave unchangedNo card-styled non-cards found

Checked every grid/list item sitewide for border+shadow+background chrome implying clickability with no actual click behaviour. None found — Home's area tiles are real links, the assessment grid and chapter list use plain rule-dividers with no card chrome, matching the site's own stated rule against unnecessary cards.

C4Previously a real problem, already resolvedMeaningless connector lines

The Operating Model's earlier curved-SVG connectors (implying a false one-to-one link between rows across the three columns) were exactly this defect — already identified and replaced this session with the axis-and-drop-line structure, which now encodes a real relationship rather than a decorative one.

C5OptionalTwo equal-width 3-up sections run back-to-back on Home

Role-row then areas-band, both exactly three equal columns. Individually justified (§C2), and different enough in treatment — rule-divided text vs. full-bleed photography — that a reader likely won't consciously register the repetition. Named for completeness, not proposed as a change.

3Colour

Co1Leave unchangedPurple-tinted neutrals are a brand choice, not an oversight

Every "grey" on the site — --ink-2/3/4, --eyebrow-ink — is a purple-grey mix, never a true achromatic grey. Read literally, "purple used for every label and divider" is true. But a generic template reaches for flat, brand-agnostic #888 greys; tinting even the neutral scale with sumire is the less generic choice, not the more generic one. Leave as is.

Co2Recommended (cross-ref Inventory audit P2-2)The one real ambient-background hit

About's "Who We Support" radial lavender wash remains the one place a gradient/third ground colour appears outside the cream/sumire system. Not re-detailed here — see the Inventory & Consistency Audit for the full entry.

Co3Leave unchangedNav backdrop-blur

Restrained (18px blur, modest opacity shift), and a near-universal modern-web convention rather than a genre-specific tell. Doesn't read as luxury-template on its own.

Co4OptionalOne of the brand's three stated "signature devices" appears to be unused live

brand.css documents three signature devices: the Frame (corner brackets — confirmed live, Home hero), colour washes (confirmed live), and "the Index" — a numbered hairline with a small diamond node (.r-index, --reiwa-node). No live page markup was found using .r-index, .r-ey or the diamond-node caption pattern the CSS comments describe. Either instantiate it somewhere, or drop it — a device that's part of the stated brand system but never actually appears reads as unfinished if anyone reopens the stylesheet later.

5Photography

Ph1RequiredFour hero images are blurred past the point of being identifiable architecture

What is wrong

Home, About, Approach and Investment Focus all use a hero image with essentially no fine detail left in the file itself (confirmed by opening each file and by their file sizes relative to their pixel dimensions — see the headline callout above for the exact numbers). About's and Approach's are the most severe: no façade, skyline or structure of any kind is identifiable — just soft colour and light. Home's is less extreme but still a heavily blurred, out-of-focus building corner.

Why it feels generic / ungrounded

A rendered result with no identifiable structure could be a blurred photo of any building anywhere, or no real photograph at all — the opposite of "specific to Reiwa Capital," and visually indistinguishable from an AI-smoothed placeholder, which the brief's own imagery rule explicitly prohibits. It cannot currently be verified either way from what's on screen.

Smallest effective correction

Replace the four hero assets with sharp source photographs graded the same way the two market photos already are (saturate(.95) contrast(1.03), the site's existing, correct treatment) — no new design work required, just correctly-detailed source files run through the grade that's already defined and already proven to work well.

What must be preserved

The scrim/gradient treatment, crop points, and aspect ratios currently tuned around these slots; the warm, low-saturation colour temperature the market photos establish.

Risk of over-correcting

None identified — this is a straightforward asset-quality fix, not a design decision with a wrong-direction risk.

Ph2Leave unchangedThe two market photographs are excellent

Sharp, specific, correctly cropped, consistent golden-hour warmth. London shows an identifiable, real skyline (Tower Bridge, the Gherkin, the Walkie-Talkie); Amsterdam shows a specific, correctly-detailed canal-house row with authentic Dutch gable architecture. No card treatment, no generic stock-photo staging. This is the standard the four hero images should be held to.

Ph3Open verification gapSix area/chapter background images not checked this pass

The Income/Repositioning/Hospitality images used on Home and Investment Focus were not opened this session. Given the hero pattern found above, worth a quick check before assuming they match the market photos' quality rather than the heroes'.

6Motion

M1Leave unchangedStagger is used three times, each independently tuned — not a blanket effect

Nav drawer items (100/140/180/220/260ms), hero elements (sub +80ms, location +140ms), and chapter reveal (text +80ms after image) are the only staggered sequences on the site. Each is scoped to its own specific content and hand-tuned, the opposite of the generic "fade up every card on scroll" library effect that shows up on most templates.

M2Leave unchangedNo parallax, pointer-following, delayed navigation or unstable height changes found

Checked craft.js and shell.js in full — no mousemove-driven effects, no scroll-tied parallax beyond the Process page's progress spine (which is informational, not decorative), no navigation that waits on an animation before the browser follows a link, and the language-swap explicitly holds body height during the crossfade to prevent a jump.

M3Leave unchangedReduced-motion is honoured without exception, sitewide

Every animated pattern found (hero fade, reveal-on-scroll, drawer stagger, market crossfade, language swap, disclosure expand) is gated behind prefers-reduced-motion and additionally backed by a forced-visible timeout, so content is never dependent on an animation completing.
